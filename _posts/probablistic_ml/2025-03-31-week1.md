## Variance of the Bernoulli Distribution

A **Bernoulli distribution** describes a random variable $X$ that takes values:

$$X = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{with probability } p \\ 0, & \text{with probability } 1 - p\end{cases}$$

### 1. Expected Value $E[X]$
The expected value (mean) is defined as:

$$E[X] = \sum_{x} x \cdot P(X = x)$$

For a Bernoulli random variable:

$$E[X] = (1 \cdot p) + (0 \cdot (1 - p)) = p$$

### 2. Expected Value of $X^2$
Since $X^2 = X$ for a Bernoulli variable (because $1^2 = 1$ and $0^2 = 0$):

$$E[X^2] = (1^2 \cdot p) + (0^2 \cdot (1 - p)) = p$$

### 3. Variance Formula
Variance is defined as:

$$\text{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$$

Substituting the values:

$$\text{Var}(X) = p - p^2$$

$$\text{Var}(X) = p(1 - p)$$

### 4. Interpretation
- The variance is highest when $p = 0.5$, meaning maximum uncertainty.
- The variance is $0$ when $p = 0$ or $p = 1$, meaning no uncertainty (constant outcomes).
- The formula $p(1 - p)$ represents the spread of probability between success $(1)$ and failure $(0)$.

Thus, the **variance of a Bernoulli distribution** is:

$$\text{Var}(X) = p(1 - p)$$
