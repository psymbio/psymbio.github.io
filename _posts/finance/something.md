# Introduction to Accounting

Accounting is the formal collection, aggregation, analysis, and reporting of financial and non-financial data about a company to various end users.

Some of this reporting is mandated and must be released periodically, typically quarterly.

## Types of Accounting

1. Financial Accounting
   - Provides information to people outside the company.
   - Primary Purpose: Inform outsiders about the company’s financial performance and health.
     - Useful for:
       - Valuation by equity investors and investment banks.
       - Credit risk assessment by banks and suppliers.
   - Secondary Purpose: Monitor and evaluate manager performance and ensure responsible resource use.
   - Communication:
     - Through financial statements and additional disclosures.
     - Included in quarterly and annual reports.
     - These reports are audited for accuracy and compliance with accounting standards.

2. Managerial Accounting
   - Provides information to people within the company.
   - Primary Purpose: Aid in internal decision-making.
     - Helps with:
       - Determining product costs, profitability, and pricing.
       - Product mix decisions.
       - Introduction/discontinuation of products.
       - Capacity planning and production volume decisions.
   - Secondary Purpose: Performance evaluation and cost control.
   - Reports:
     - No fixed formats.
     - Not audited.

3. Tax Accounting
   - Estimates taxes to be paid and ensures compliance with tax disclosures.
   - Provides information to tax authorities.
   - Legal to prepare separate financial and tax statements, as rules may differ between them.

> In this course, the focus will be only on Financial Accounting.

## Accrual Accounting

A key element of financial accounting is the idea of Accruals.

- Financial statements are based on accrual accounting rather than cash basis.
- Transactions are recognized when the primary economic event occurs, not when the cash flow happens.
- This allows verification of earnings and the net financial position of the company during a period.

### Key Processes in Accrual Accounting

- Revenue Recognition: 
  - Revenue is recorded when:
    - A product or service is delivered,
    - It can be measured reliably,
    - Receipt of payment is reasonably certain.

- Matching Costs to Revenues:
  - Expenses directly related to revenue are recorded in the same period as the revenue.
  - Expenses not directly related to revenue are recognized over the time period in which their benefits are realized.

> In essence, accrual accounting means you don't need to see the cash to record a transaction.

### Advantages and Limitations

- Advantages:
  - Financial statements reflect transactions when the economic event occurs.
  - Provides timelier and more decision-relevant information.

- Limitations:
  - Based on judgments and estimates, so less objectively reliable than cash flows.
  - As the saying goes:  
    > *"Cash flow is a fact, earnings is a matter of opinion."*

Because of this, financial accounting is guided by well-specified rules, but there is room for managerial discretion, making external audits essential.
